Is It Your Hormones?

Estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone all decline as women age, and the changes can start in your late 30s or 40s, years before your last period. Common signs include:

  • Hot flashes and night sweats
  • Trouble falling or staying asleep
  • Low libido or vaginal dryness
  • Brain fog, forgetfulness, or trouble focusing
  • Anxiety, irritability, or low mood
  • Fatigue that sleep doesn’t fix
  • Weight gain around the middle and loss of muscle tone
  • Thinning hair or dry skin
  • Irregular or heavier periods in perimenopause

If several sound familiar, a hormone lab panel is the best place to start.

Hormone Therapy at Every Stage

Perimenopause (often late 30s to early 50s)

Hormones begin to fluctuate unpredictably. Periods may become irregular, and symptoms like poor sleep, anxiety, and heavier cycles often appear first.

Menopause and Postmenopause

Once periods have stopped for 12 months, estrogen and testosterone levels stay low. Hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, and bone loss become more common.

After a Hysterectomy or Surgical Menopause

Removing the ovaries causes an abrupt drop in hormones, and symptoms can be sudden and intense. Hormone therapy is often considered to ease the transition.

Which Hormones Are Used for Women?

Your plan is built from your labs and symptoms, and may include:

  • Estradiol: the main form of estrogen, which helps with hot flashes, sleep, vaginal health, and bone protection
  • Testosterone: in small doses for women, which can support energy, libido, mood, and muscle tone
  • Progesterone: for women who still have a uterus and are taking estrogen, progesterone (usually an oral capsule) is prescribed to protect the uterine lining

Pellets deliver estradiol and testosterone. Progesterone is prescribed separately when needed.

  • 2–3 months: improved libido and overall sense of well-being

Is Biote Safe for Women?

Hormone therapy can be a good option for many women, especially those under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, but it isn’t right for everyone. Biote pellets are compounded preparations and are not FDA-approved as finished products, though the hormones they contain are used in FDA-approved medications. Hormone therapy may not be suitable if you have a history of:

  • Breast or uterine cancer
  • Blood clots or stroke
  • Heart disease
  • Liver disease
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding

Before starting, we make sure your mammogram and Pap test are up to date and review your personal and family history. Possible side effects include insertion-site soreness, acne, hair changes, breast tenderness, and mood shifts, which we monitor at every follow-up.
