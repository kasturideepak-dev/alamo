What Is Tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide is the first dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist, a medication that mimics two natural gut hormones involved in appetite and blood sugar. It’s the active ingredient in Zepbound, which is FDA-approved for chronic weight management and for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity, and Mounjaro, which is approved for type 2 diabetes. Your provider will explain which form and dose is right for you.

How Tirzepatide Works: Two Hormones, One Weekly Shot

This dual action is why tirzepatide often produces greater average weight loss than single-hormone medications.

  • GLP-1 actionReduces appetite, slows digestion, and helps you feel full sooner
  • GIP actionWorks alongside GLP-1 to further improve appetite control and how your body handles blood sugar and fat
  • Steadier blood sugarSupports insulin release when blood sugar rises
  • Less food noiseMany patients report fewer cravings and less constant thinking about food

Tirzepatide Results

In the SURMOUNT-1 clinical trial, adults taking the highest dose of tirzepatide (15 mg) alongside lifestyle changes lost about 20% of their body weight on average over 72 weeks. In a head-to-head trial, tirzepatide led to greater average weight loss than semaglutide. Results vary from person to person.

At Alamo Primary Care, we track your progress with monthly visits and InBody body composition scans, so you can see that the weight coming off is fat, not muscle.

Who Is Tirzepatide For?

Tirzepatide is generally considered for adults who have:

  • A BMI of 30 or higher, or
  • A BMI of 27 or higher with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol, or obstructive sleep apnea
  • It may be a strong option if you’ve tried other approaches, including semaglutide, without the results you wanted

Tirzepatide may not be right for you if you have:

  • A personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • A history of pancreatitis
  • Pregnancy, plans to become pregnant, or are breastfeeding
  • Severe digestive conditions such as gastroparesis

Important for women: tirzepatide can make birth control pills less effective. If you use oral contraceptives, your provider will recommend a backup or non-oral method for four weeks after starting and after each dose increase.

Common Side Effects

Most side effects are digestive and tend to ease over time.

  • Nausea
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Indigestion or bloating
  • Decreased appetite
  • Mild injection-site reactions

Tips that help: eat smaller, slower meals, stop at the first sign of fullness, prioritize protein, limit greasy foods, and stay well hydrated. Contact us right away for severe stomach pain, persistent vomiting, or signs of an allergic reaction.
