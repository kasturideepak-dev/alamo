# Alamo Primary Care — design prototype

Two pages for client approval, built as static HTML/CSS/JS (no build step, no Next.js).
Once the direction is approved, this becomes the reference for the Next.js build.

## Pages

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | **Portal / landing page** — the group gateway. Centred-logo nav, big serif headline, 4 service cards (Primary Care, Weight Loss, Hormone Replacement, Aesthetics), San Antonio / Boerne location switcher. |
| `primary-care.html` | **Primary Care homepage** — full-bleed photographic hero with an overlay header, value strip, centred About statement, marquee, navy service carousel, about + stats, three pillars, provider, testimonials, CTA band, footer. |

Open either file directly in a browser, or run a local server from this folder:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 4321
```

Add `?static=1` to any URL to disable all motion (useful for screenshots, PDF export,
or reviewing layout without waiting for scroll animations).

## Design system

**Every colour on both pages is one of the two colours in the logo artwork, at a different
lightness.** No third hue is introduced anywhere.

| | Sampled from the logo | HSL |
|---|---|---|
| Navy | `#2A3078` — the ALAMO wordmark | H 235.4° · S 48.1% · L 31.8% |
| Green | `#52B04F` — PRIMARY CARE and the emblem | H 118.1° · S 38.0% · L 50.0% |

Each token in `assets/css/style.css` is generated by holding that hue and saturation and moving
only lightness — so `--navy-900` through `--navy-50`, the action scale, the greens, the page
background, the ink, the muted text, the hairlines and the card washes are all literally the logo,
lighter or darker.

- **The action colour is the logo navy itself** (`--blue-500: #2A3078`), with `--blue-600` a
  darker step for hover. Buttons are the brand colour, not an approximation of it.
- **Green marks interaction and confirmation**: the icon circle on every button hover, the border
  and fill on outlined buttons, the carousel arrows, the tick marks in the About list, the
  accordion's open row, and the footer contact icons (6.97:1 on the footer ground).
- Service cards alternate two depths of the same navy (L 13% and L 44%) rather than two hues.
- Contrast checked: buttons 11.7:1, headings 16.6:1, eyebrows 6.1:1, body 5.9:1, green ticks 4.7:1.
  The one value under target is the white arrow on the green hover circle at 2.7:1 — decorative
  only, and it sits beside a fully legible label, but say the word and I will deepen that circle
  to `--green-600` (5.6:1) at the cost of a slightly less vivid green.

**Texture.** Light sections carry a fine dot grid (`.tex`) — a 26px radial-gradient lattice in
the logo navy at 7% alpha, masked to fade out at the top and bottom so section edges stay crisp.
It sits at `z-index: -1` inside an `isolation: isolate` context, so it renders above the section's
background but behind all content. `.tex--glow` adds a soft corner tint for depth. Applied to the
About, Why, Providers and Blog sections, and to the portal page.

Other foundations:

- **Fraunces** (display serif) echoes the serif in the ALAMO wordmark; **Inter** for UI and body.
- Tokens live at the top of `assets/css/style.css` — change them there, not inline.
- `.scard` / `.svc-grid` are the portal's card component. They were once shared with the Primary
  Care services grid; that grid became the navy carousel, so the portal is now their only
  consumer. Do not delete them when editing the services section.
- Responsive from 360px up. Nav collapses to a full-screen drawer below 980px.
- Vertical rhythm: `.section` is `clamp(58px, 6.4vw, 100px)` top and bottom, `.section--tight`
  is `clamp(44px, 4.6vw, 72px)`. Change these two, not individual sections. `.why` and
  `.stats-band` carry smaller values of their own because the stats card overlaps between them.

## About section

Follows the Clireo reference: a centred eyebrow with a dot, one large statement carrying the
scrub + blur reveal, a pill CTA paired with a trust cluster, then three icon cards.

- The statement is the client's own Figma sentence, not new copy.
- The three cards each carry a small visual, and every one is built from a real Figma fact rather
  than invented data: three named service rows — thumbnail plus service name, with "+ 9 more
  services" carrying the count to twelve. Photos alone at thumbnail size were unreadable and
  numbered chips said nothing, so each image is labelled. An avatar cluster with `+16` reading "20+ board certified", and a chip row for
  same-day / telemedicine / insurance / ages 16+. Rows slide in on a stagger on hover. The third is a feature card with a photographic ground
  (`clinic-interior.jpg`) under a navy-to-green gradient veil, a CTA, and a pointer-tracked drift
  — the photo leans ~6% opposite the cursor on pointer-fine devices. Swapping the `<img>` for a
  muted autoplay `<video>` would work unchanged if the client supplies footage.
- No charts or percentages were invented. The reference's dashboard look comes from data a clinic
  dashboard would own; everything here maps to a number the client actually gave us.
- `prov-1..4.jpg` are clinician faces cropped from `s09-referrals.jpg`. Stock, like the patient
  avatars — replace with real staff photography before launch.
- Card icons use the logo green; the pill CTA's square arrow goes green on hover, as buttons do.
- The trust avatars are faces cropped from three of the client's own patient photographs
  (`s07-mens`, `s02-chronic`, `s04-checkups`). They illustrate "5,000+ patients" generically and
  are attributed to no one — but swap them for real patient or staff photography before launch.
- This replaced the old two-column About; the clinic interior and monument-sign photographs are
  no longer used on the page.

## Why Choose Us

Replaces the three navy pillar cards. Pill eyebrow, a two-tone heading, then a five-row accordion
beside a portrait — following the reference.

- The accordion opens one row at a time **on hover** (pointer-fine devices) as well as on click
  and keyboard focus, animating height with GSAP while the copy fades up behind it. Tweens use
  `overwrite: true` so sweeping the cursor down the list does not stack animations. First row open
  by default; falls back to plain show/hide under reduced motion and on touch.
- The heading block is centred; the accordion and photo below it stay left/right.
- The heading is two-tone *and* keeps its scrub reveal, which needed the text splitter rewritten:
  it now walks child nodes instead of reading `textContent`, so an inline `<span class="hl">`
  keeps its class on each of its words.
- The five rows restate facts already in the Figma — board-certified team, in-house diagnostics,
  twelve services, personalised plans, unhurried setting.
- Image is `waiting-room-b.jpg`, the client's real community room. The studio cutouts float in a
  rounded frame; a photograph with a real background does not.

## Stats overlap

The stats card sits between Why Choose Us and the providers band, straddling the boundary between
the two background colours. `.stats-band` carries a negative bottom margin equal to `--overlap`
(`clamp(52px, 6vw, 92px)`) and `z-index: 2`; `.providers-sec` adds the same value back as top
padding so its content is not pushed under the card. Measured 86px of overlap at 1440, 52px at
390. The card's shadow was softened and widened so it reads as lifted off the colour change
rather than stuck to it.

## Testimonials

Three review cards: star rating, a short title, the quote, then avatar + name + service. Replaced
the single rotating quote with its arrows and dots, so all three read at once.

## Section backgrounds

No two adjacent sections share a ground, so each reads as its own block:

`paper → navy-800 → navy-50 → paper → navy-800 → paper → navy-50 → navy-800 → paper → navy-900`

Testimonials sit on `--navy-800` with white cards — the white pops hardest there, and it separates
the section from the providers band above. The marquee is on `--paper`: it previously matched the
About band above it, and because the band is masked to fade at both ends, matching the page ground
also makes those edges seamless. If you add a section, check this sequence before picking its
background.

## Typography — orphans

Headings use `text-wrap: balance`, which is capped at ~6 lines in Chrome, so the long About
statement falls back to `text-wrap: pretty` below 620px. Where balance cannot help — a two-word
phrase that will not fit — the words are bound with `&nbsp;`, and the text splitter now breaks on
ordinary whitespace only (`/[ \t\r\n]+/`) rather than `/\s+/`, which matches U+00A0 in JS and was
tearing those pairs apart again. Verified zero single-word last lines across 33 headings at ten
widths from 1920 down to 360.

## Blog

Three article cards above the CTA — image, date, category tag, title — with the heading left and
the supporting line right. **Every post is placeholder**: the titles, dates and categories are
mine. Thumbnails reuse service photographs chosen to match each topic.

## CTA band

Full-bleed: the band spans the viewport with no rounding, while the glass panel stays aligned to
the shell grid. Rebuilt on the "featured post" pattern: a sharp photograph with a frosted glass panel inset on
the left, rather than the previous blanket overlay that flattened the whole image.

- Panel is `rgba(navy, .58)` over `backdrop-filter: blur(20px)`, with a hairline light border.
  The photo behind it stays unblurred and full contrast.
- One solid white primary button plus a quiet outline for the phone number, so there is a single
  obvious action.
- `cta-visit.jpg` is a landscape crop of the team photograph; `cta-visit-mobile.jpg` is a portrait
  crop of the same frame, served below 700px via `<picture>`. On mobile the panel anchors to the
  bottom with the photo reading above it — a full-width panel over a landscape crop just hid the
  image entirely.

## Marquee and services

Both follow the Clireo reference.

**Marquee** — a light band of service names separated by a sparkle glyph, masked to fade at both
edges. The run of words is cloned in JS until it covers twice the viewport, then `x` wraps through
a GSAP `modifiers` function, so the loop is seamless at any width. It runs at ~165px/sec — at this type size
anything slower reads as static. A ScrollTrigger feeds scroll velocity into `timeScale`, so the band leans into the direction you are scrolling and eases back
to normal speed 130ms after you stop.

**Services** — the 12 service cards sit on a navy band that **pins to the viewport** while the
track travels sideways in step with the wheel; when the last card lands the pin releases and the
page carries on to the stats. Built with `ScrollTrigger` (`pin: true`, `scrub: 0.8`,
`invalidateOnRefresh`), with the travel distance measured live from the first and last card so
the last one finishes inset by the same gutter the first starts at — it stays correct on resize.

- The section is sized to `100svh` on desktop, with a `max-height: 820px` tier that tightens the
  padding and heading, because a pinned section taller than the viewport gets clipped.
- Card size is driven by `height: clamp(300px, min(40svh, 26vw), 520px)` with `aspect-ratio`
  supplying the width. Sizing off viewport *height* stops the band from stranding 280px of empty
  navy above and below on tall screens; the `26vw` bound keeps three cards in the row as screens
  get wider.
- The foot is two clusters — supporting line left, then `Talk to our team` + divider + arrows
  right — rather than three, which left the arrows floating alone mid-row.
- The prev/next arrows now nudge the *page* scroll by one card; since scroll maps 1:1 to card
  travel inside the pin, one click moves exactly one card.
- A separate ScrollTrigger rises and fades the cards in (0.09s stagger) as the band arrives. It
  animates the cards, never the track, so it cannot fight the horizontal tween.
- The pin carries `refreshPriority: 1`. A pinned trigger moves every trigger below it; without
  that, the scrub headings after this section computed their start positions ~3700px too early
  and revealed entirely off screen. There is also a `ScrollTrigger.refresh()` on `load` and on
  `document.fonts.ready`, since both shift layout.
- Below 981px, and under reduced motion at any width, the pin is skipped entirely and the track
  becomes a native scroll-snap swipe (`.tcarousel.is-free`). Hijacking scroll on touch is worse
  than useless.

Note this replaced the 4x3 grid the client picked earlier. The card treatment is unchanged —
photo, brand wash, title, description — but only three are visible at a time instead of all
twelve. If the client wants the full set visible at a glance, the grid is a quick revert.

## Mega menu

A **contained panel**, not a full-bleed band: `width: min(1300px, 100vw - 2 gutters)`, centred
under the header with rounded corners and a shadow, so it reads as a floating card like the rest
of the page. Three columns — twelve services flowing down two, Explore, and a preview card — plus
a footer row carrying a supporting line and a CTA.

Below 1100px the preview card is hidden and the freed width goes to the service list, which takes
label wrapping to zero at those sizes. One long label ("Routine Check-ups & Preventative Care", 37
characters) still wraps at 1280–1440; widening the panel only shifts which label wraps, and
shrinking the type costs more than it gains, so that one is left alone.

- Every link has an icon chip that fills green and tilts on hover, with a sliding arrow.
- **Hovering a service crossfades the preview card** to that service's photograph and caption
  (GSAP, `overwrite: true` so sweeping the list does not stack tweens). Keyboard focus does the
  same, so it is not mouse-only.
- Opening staggers the columns then the links, driven by a `MutationObserver` on the item's
  `is-open` class, so the CSS open/close stays the single source of truth.

## Header and hero

Modelled on the Elder Care reference, with one deliberate departure: **the logo stays centred**,
because that is a stated client preference. Everything else follows the reference —

- A thin accent line across the very top, deep logo navy to light logo navy.
- A dark gradient veil behind the floating header (opaque at the top edge, gone by 230px) so the
  utility row and nav always read over bright photography. It fades out as the header goes solid.
- The header floats transparently over a full-bleed hero: white nav, the active item in a
  translucent pill, a solid logo-navy CTA, and a **reversed white lockup** (`logo-white.png`) that
  crossfades to the colour lockup once the header goes solid — no white chip behind the logo.
- Past 64px of scroll it becomes the solid blurred light bar, the utility row collapsing to zero
  height as it goes.
- The hero is a full-viewport photograph with a two-axis scrim, an announcement pill, the
  headline, and two frosted-glass service cards offset over the image.
- Hero photography: `hero-full.jpg` (2400px) with a portrait `hero-full-mobile.jpg` crop served
  below 700px via `<picture>`.

## Motion

GSAP 3.13 + ScrollTrigger + Lenis smooth scroll, all served locally from `assets/js/`
so the prototype works offline. No WebGL.

- **Scroll-scrubbed text reveal** (`data-reveal="scrub"`) — words brighten from 16% to full
  opacity tied to scroll position, not a timer. Applied to the "Healthcare that's personal,
  modern and trusted" heading. Add the attribute to any element to reuse it.
- Hero image scrubs scale and drift as it leaves the viewport
- Word-by-word headline reveals (custom splitter, no paid plugin)
- Scrub reveals lock once complete, and `lock()` guards against being called from `onUpdate`
  *during* `ScrollTrigger.create()` — which happens when the page loads already scrolled past a
  heading (reload, hash jump, back/forward). Unguarded it threw, aborting the init loop and
  leaving every later reveal at `opacity: 0`. A `load` sweep also force-shows any reveal still
  invisible, so a future error can never blank a section.
- Clip-path image reveals with scroll parallax. Parallaxed images rest at `scale: 1.14` rather
  than 1 — at scale 1 the ±5% translate uncovered the frame behind the image, leaving a visible
  strip along the top edge.
- Staggered section reveals, animated stat counters
- Cursor-following image preview on the 12-service list
- Every `.btn` carries a GSAP hover timeline (button scale, icon scale + rotate, press state) plus
  the green icon-circle accent;
  those tagged `data-magnetic` also lean toward the cursor. Pointer-fine devices only.
- Floating hero cards drift on a loop
- Everything is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion` and under `?static=1`

## Assets

Images in `assets/img/` were extracted from the client's Figma file. The file turned out to
contain **134 images**, most of them high-resolution originals — the first pass only surfaced
the low-res placements, so every photo on both pages has now been replaced with the full-size
version (hero, provider, building, clinic interiors and all 12 service photos, up to 4000px
before optimisation).

Two exceptions still need the client:

- `logo.png` has been keyed to a transparent background (the Figma export was opaque white, which
  showed as a patch behind the logo) and `logo-white.png` is a reversed lockup derived from it.
- **`logo.png` is 264x67** — the only clean version in the file. There is a 2172x724 reversed
  variant, but it is a poorly-keyed trace with visible artefacts, so it is unusable. **We need
  the vector (SVG/AI/EPS)** for the centred-logo nav.
- `s12-immunization.jpg` (753x407) and `s03-screenings.jpg` (676x454) are the largest versions
  of those two photos in the file. Fine at card size; replace if the cards ever grow.

## Service cards

The 12-service grid follows the treatment the client picked from Primary Care 2 — photographic
cards with alternating navy/green brand washes in a checkerboard — rebuilt rather than copied:

- The wash is a multi-stop gradient that clears the top of the photo, instead of the flat
  half-and-half colour band in the Figma.
- Hover lifts the card, zooms and re-saturates the photo, deepens and raises the wash, reveals
  the one-line description and an arrow, fades in the service number and draws a hairline ring.
- Below 560px the description and arrow are always visible, since there is no hover on touch.
- The same card component (`.scard--lg`) is reused for the four portal cards, so both pages
  share one system.
