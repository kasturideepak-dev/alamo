What Is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, a medication that mimics a natural gut hormone your body releases after eating. It’s the active ingredient in Wegovy, which is FDA-approved for chronic weight management, and Ozempic, which is approved for type 2 diabetes. Your provider will explain which form and dose is appropriate for you.

How Semaglutide Works

  • Reduces appetiteSignals the brain that you’re full, so you naturally eat less
  • Slows digestionFood stays in the stomach longer, helping you feel satisfied
  • Supports blood sugar controlHelps the body release insulin when blood sugar rises
  • Reduces food noiseMany patients notice fewer cravings and less constant thinking about food

Who Is Semaglutide For?

Semaglutide is generally considered for adults who have:

  • A BMI of 30 or higher, or
  • A BMI of 27 or higher with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol, or sleep apnea

Semaglutide may not be right for you if you have:

  • A personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • A history of pancreatitis
  • Pregnancy, plans to become pregnant, or are breastfeeding
  • Certain gallbladder or kidney conditions

Your provider will confirm eligibility through your history and labs.

Your Semaglutide Dosing Schedule

Semaglutide is started at a low dose and increased gradually, usually every four weeks, to help your body adjust and limit side effects.

What Results Can You Expect?

In major clinical trials, adults taking semaglutide 2.4 mg alongside lifestyle changes lost about 15% of their body weight on average over roughly 68 weeks. Results vary from person to person. Most patients notice reduced appetite within the first few weeks, with steadier weight loss as the dose increases.

At Alamo Primary Care, we track progress with monthly visits and InBody body composition scans, so you can see that you’re losing fat, not muscle.

Common Side Effects

Most side effects are digestive and tend to ease as your body adjusts.

  • Nausea
  • Constipation or diarrhea
  • Bloating or indigestion
  • Reduced appetite
  • Fatigue in the early weeks

Tips that help: eat smaller meals, stop eating when you feel full, limit greasy or very sweet foods, and stay hydrated. Contact us right away for severe stomach pain, persistent vomiting, or signs of an allergic reaction.

Semaglutide vs. Tirzepatide

Semaglutide acts on one appetite hormone receptor (GLP-1). Tirzepatide acts on two (GIP and GLP-1). Both are once-weekly injections.

Is semaglutide the same as Ozempic and Wegovy?

Yes, semaglutide is the active ingredient in both. Wegovy is FDA-approved for chronic weight management at doses up to 2.4 mg, while Ozempic is approved for type 2 diabetes. Your Alamo Primary Care provider will explain which option fits your health and coverage.

How quickly does semaglutide work?

Who qualifies for semaglutide for weight loss?
